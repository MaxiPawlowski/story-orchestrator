Story Orchestrator UI Rework

Five reviewers went through every screen on test lanes, on desktop and at phone width, and read the code behind each one. The verdict matches your feeling: too many accordions, settings scattered across pages, and a visual style that ignores the SillyTavern theme it sits in. Below is what they measured, the direction I recommend, rough mockups in ST's default dark theme, and the decisions that are yours.

Already in progress, no decision needed

Two agents are fixing what every direction needs, each on its own branch, merged only with the full gates green.

  • styleReal status colours and theme-following backgrounds, built from ST's own variables. The settings styles now apply in the story window. A tidy Extensions block that collapses like the other extensions. Status shown as icon, colour and word. A test that blocks hard-coded colours.
  • bugsPer-chat judge scope; a real scene name in the bar; the chip legend no longer breaking the row and raw JSON out of the chat; one header on Help with links that point at real pages; a clear message in one-on-one chats; the relationship-key validation check; no-model test lanes no longer calling remote models.

The direction

  • 1Look native, but modern. Every colour comes from your ST theme, plus four status colours that read on light and dark. One small kit of parts (button, switch, segmented control, setting row, tabs, side menu, card, badge, empty state, dialog) replaces the mix of styles.
  • 2Side menus instead of accordions. Settings, the Author page and Studio each get a left menu of flat sections. Descriptions are plain text, not hidden behind "?". At most one sub-page, never an accordion inside an accordion.
  • 3Each setting lives in one place, and search finds it. One search box over every setting jumps to it and highlights it. Elsewhere you see the value read-only with a "Change" link.
  • 4One story sheet beside the chat. Journal, stats, people and places, ideas and memory become tabs on one sheet docked to the right. On a phone it's a bottom sheet that keeps the last reply visible.
  • 5Play is a story page; Author is a run console. Play opens on the scene, then one "things need attention" line. The Author page gets a fixed steering column, sections named by the question they answer, and an inspector that opens beside the chat.

Decisions for you

Each has my recommendation. Reply "yes to all" to take them, or name the ones to change.

1Accent colourRecommended: follow your theme's quote colour, so we always match ST.Or a fixed Story Orchestrator accent mixed into the theme.
2On/off controlsRecommended: switches for on/off settings, segmented buttons for small choices, checkboxes only for picking several items from a list.Or ST-style checkboxes everywhere.
3DensityRecommended: compact 36 px rows with a mouse, 44 px on touch.Or 44 px everywhere, as the story window does today.
4Story window on desktopRecommended: keep the dialog, but translucent with ST's blur and a content width of about 1,200 px.Or a side drawer with the chat visible behind it, or opaque full-screen as now.
5Story sheet in chatRecommended: one tabbed sheet docked right of the chat, poppable; bottom sheet on a phone.Or keep separate floating panels for journal, stats and ideas.
6Notes under replies (player level)Recommended: one quiet line of words, like "↳ The story moved on · Remembered 2 things", that expands on click.Or keep icon chips, but with labels.
7Names and player navigationRecommended: the two halves become "This chat" and "Settings". Players see Play and Settings only, and the Author view switch lives in Settings.Or keep "This story" / "Orchestrator" and the current tabs.
8Per-device display settingsRecommended: keep them, for phone versus PC, but as a small "this device" chip on the setting instead of a second list.Or drop them and keep install-wide values only.
9Start pane orderRecommended: the briefing first, then who you play as one control, then one Start button.Or choose your identity first, as now.
10Author and Studio layoutRecommended: a steering column on the left, the inspector beside the chat, a left menu in Studio, one page per character, and Offers edited as rows instead of JSON.Or keep top tabs flattened into one row, and the Roster grouped by feature.
11Expert settingsRecommended: always reachable on sub-pages (per-task model routes, picture types). No hidden "expert mode".Or a single "Show expert settings" switch.
